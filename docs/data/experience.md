    > I'm studying for a bachelor's degree in economics and business administration, now in my third year and taking the economic analysis profile. I have just taken part in a BIP, Current Issues in Management & Finance, a blended Erasmus+ programme held in Budapest, Hungary, lasting under a week. The programme was built around many different lectures, and you could choose the topics you wanted. I have also taken part in another BIP in Italy, at the University of Chieti-Pescara.
    >
    > Before the stay we got a short digital introduction to the programme, the lectures and the studies. The first day began with a joint welcome and ice-breakers before the lectures started. Almost every day you could choose between two lectures and tailor the timetable to what interested you most, so you learn a lot not only from the strong academic content but also from the lecturers' different backgrounds. The lectures that taught me the most were "How do we make strategic decisions" and "Effects of central bank digital currencies". In the first, we learned that the choices we make are not really our own, but have already been made by others.
    >
    > On the Thursday there was a social Easter event with Hungarian food, Easter traditions, a photo booth and, as the highlight, a Hungarian folk-dance performance. It was a good opportunity to build new relationships and strengthen existing ones.
    >
    > On the last day we were to present one of the topics from the lectures. We had misunderstood slightly and expected a whole morning to work on it, possibly with students from other schools, so we ended up working together as the HiØ group. That suited the organisers well, since they wanted a perspective from each participant's home country. We chose central bank digital currencies as the topic where we could add the strongest perspective, and shared insight into Norway's openness and its fast adoption of new technology. Central bank digital currencies are digital money issued by the central bank, either directly to citizens or indirectly through the banks, as in today's system. From the lecturer's research we saw that giving citizens direct access to the central bank reduces banks' capacity to lend, which can hold back economic growth, and so we learned why Norway's current system matters.
    >
    > We were also lucky enough to be there for the final result of the Hungarian election, which was unlike anything I had experienced before. There was a mood of relief, freedom and joy among the locals, who could finally move away from the drift toward an authoritarian state under Orbán. Whether it leads to a better situation for the population remains to be seen, but one can only hope for the best.
    >
    > There is a lot to learn on a BIP like this: we learned a great deal academically, learned about the local culture, met new people, witnessed an important turning point for a society, and came to appreciate the advantages of living in Norway. Short-term exchanges like this offer a lot of learning and valuable experiences that every student should take part in, and that I wouldn't have been without.
